# Experience

The scene comes first. The assistant comes second. Controls appear when the task needs them.
The interface should be quiet: a few precise marks, readable type, and no dashboard.

## A normal session

1. The person saves a scene and says what they want later. That saves the request. It does
   not start a lesson.
2. They open the scene and select a thing, or the whole view. A segment and a short label
   appear. There is no mode menu and no form for renaming.
3. The assistant returns one useful interaction tied to that subject. Speech is optional and
   captioned. Waiting is silent.
4. The person answers, picks, confirms, or does the task. A correction is given only when the
   answer needs one.
5. The result should continue with the same assistant. Until that delivery exists, the
   interface says it was not sent.

The interface stays in the person's language.

## What the app does today

One grounded subject and its label can sit in the room. The spoken line, choices, and settings
are on a console under the companion. Live proposed names are visible and not tappable.
Under the console, one row holds at most three tiles of one style, Pause always in the middle:
Talk, Pause and More, which shows Context, Pause and Back in its place, Settings on the Context
sheet. The Context sheet and the reader sit on the line's column, the same text width as the
line. While recording it holds Finish, Pause and Cancel, and while Boopy works, Stop and Pause.
After a restart before Boopy heard back about a capture, the console says "Earlier capture" with
one sentence, and the row holds Check, Pause and New.

While a turn is with the Mac, the line shows one of Boopy's thinking phrases, such as
"Hmm, let me see…", with its shimmer moving from the first frame. The next phrase follows
about every three seconds, and none repeats until all have shown. With reduced motion the
phrase changes with a cut and does not shimmer. The line shows no timer, and the phrases are
never spoken. The Mac still records each stage of the turn in its journal; the line does not
show those stage words. An answer to a task reads "Saving on your Mac…" instead.

After a first turn about a grounded subject, the headset asks the Mac for the session's task.
A first turn that offers a choice opens no task. The panel shows the prompt with one option, and
Choose sends the option shown. If it stops or fails before Boopy answers, the choice stays, unless
the Mac says the choice is no longer available. Follow-up questions page behind Boopy's line, each
with Ask.
The task uses the same parts: the chip on the outlined subject and Boopy's line on the console.
The line's speaker reads "Boopy · Not sent". Only while the line asks the person something is the
row's first tile Answer, with Talk's mic and labeled with the prompt the Mac sends, in Talk's
place. Once the line shows the person's answer, or when the line asks nothing, Talk is the first
tile again.
A task without its chip or its line, with no answer and not marked wrong item, is not shown:
the conversation keeps its own line and chip. A wrong item clears the chip and the outline and
says "Wrong item."
Answering aloud records the words on the task, on the Mac. While the answer saves, the line
reads "Saving on your Mac…". The line then shows those words
exactly, under "You, as heard · Not sent", because the Mac's recognizer gives no confidence.
If the task changed on the Mac before the answer arrived, the answer is not recorded: the
headset shows the Mac's task and says "That answer was not recorded." If it cannot read the
Mac's task then, it says "That answer was not recorded. Reconnect before answering again."
Nothing reaches the person's own assistant. A later Talk turn
returns to the conversation's own line and follow-up questions. Without a task, the
conversation looks as before.

Arrange-and-sequence activities have service support and no scene renderer. Do not add a tray
or a stack of cards for them.

The companion can be quiet, near the conversation, or present for a longer exchange. It does
not have to appear for every selection, and it must not cover the subject. The current orb
follows listening, thinking, and errors. It is one character, not a cast of helpers.

## Correction

A wrong label is rare. "Wrong item", or selecting again, drops the content that depended on
that label. It does not rewrite the saved image, and it does not ask the person to type a
replacement name.

## Copy

Use short status lines: Checking. Can't read the label. Wrong item. Try again. Not sent.
Still checking. Continue later.

Do not say "definitely." While Boopy works, one line of short, playful thinking words shows,
with no timer.

## Comfort

Keep the selected target still while a hand is on it. Keep unfinished input when the layout
changes. Prefer text that faces the wearer. Long text belongs on a surface the person opened.

Hit targets are at least 48 dp, and about 60 dp for a primary hand target, including invisible
padding when the mark itself is smaller. Offer reduced motion and a way through that does not
require speech.

These sizes still need a worn check before they are treated as final.
